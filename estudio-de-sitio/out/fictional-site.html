Financing the AI part and when it pays back, per grid MW moved to AI

On a 5-year loan the rent does not cover the payment. How many of the 20 MW go to AI is set by your cash: MW = your cash / $1.76M or more.

Default structure from USD.AI: loan = 80% of capex, you put 20% plus a 3-month debt-service reserve, equal monthly principal from first rent, 1.15x minimum coverage. USD.AI lends 3 to 5 years against GPUs, not buildings; 10 years is the base term of the TeraWulf and Cipher hosting leases. Rates 10% to 15%: what USD.AI charged smaller operators (American Compute, Oct 2026). Ranges run from favorable (low capex, 85% NOI margin) to unfavorable (high capex, 70% NOI margin). Your cash before first rent = 20% of capex + 3-month reserve + interest during construction + mining given up during construction. Below 1.0x the rent does not cover the payment and you add cash every month until the loan is paid; the payback counts that. Construction 12 months: Cipher signed Barber Lake on 25 Sep 2025 with rent from Oct 2026; TeraWulf signed on 14 Aug 2025 and delivered CB-3 in early Jul 2026, and its CB-5 slipped from 2H 2026 to early 2027. Ramp 6 months: TeraWulf's plan, 40 of 200 MW in 1H 2026 and all by year-end. These are large public operators; a 20 MW site has no public benchmark. Interest during construction assumes the loan is drawn evenly over the 12 months and interest is paid monthly (no source for a small site's draw schedule). The AI MW stop mining at signing and give up $48 per MW per day, the average of the last 8 months. Rent is net of site costs: NOI margin 85% (TeraWulf, Cipher) to ~70% (TeraWulf Abernathy, average over lease life); NOI = rent less property operating expenses, property taxes and insurance. Not included: income taxes, depreciation, and the reserve coming back at the end.
